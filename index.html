Bernd Dudzik

Head of the Cognitive-Affective Technology (CAT) Lab at TU Delft

I am an Assistant Professor in the Pattern Recognition & Bioinformatics group at Delft University of Technology, where I lead the Cognitive-Affective Technology (CAT) Lab. I work at the intersection of artificial intelligence, human-computer interaction, and cognitive and social psychology.

My research explores how intelligent systems can make empathic inferences: estimating how people think, feel, and evaluate the situations they are in from multimodal sensor data. The thread running through this work is context-sensitivity and subjectivity. The same smile, pause, or heartbeat can mean different things for different people in different situations, and systems that ignore this fail once they leave the lab. I am interested in what it takes, technically and conceptually, to move such models into unconstrained, real-world settings using ubiquitous and wearable sensing.

With my team in the CAT Lab I ground this research in application domains such as healthcare and digital public services, with the aim of supporting human abilities, for example personal memory, and human-AI collaboration.
