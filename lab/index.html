CAT Lab

The Cognitive-Affective Technology Lab at TU Delft

The Cognitive-Affective Technology (CAT) Lab is a small research team in the Pattern Recognition & Bioinformatics group at Delft University of Technology, led by Bernd Dudzik.

We work on computational systems that use multimodal sensor data to interpret human behavior and relate meaningfully to how people think and feel. What sets our work apart is a focus on context and subjectivity: we study both the technical and the conceptual problems that arise because the meaning of behavior depends on who is acting, in which situation, and with which history. Our work sits at the intersection of computer science, cognitive and social psychology, and human-computer interaction.

Research lines

Our projects are organised in three connected research lines that share a common technical and methodological foundation and feed into human-centered AI applications.

Situation Evaluations & Affect

How do people evaluate the situations they are in, and how does that shape what they feel and do? We build models that estimate such evaluations and the resulting affective states from multimodal behavior, for example how people perceive potential collaboration partners during a conversation. We are extending this work from conversational settings to unconstrained everyday environments using ubiquitous sensing, including a new focus on olfaction.

Memory Processing & Recollection

Personal memories colour how people experience media, conversations, and each other. We study how memory processes such as recollection and memorability show up in behavior, how they interact with emotion, and how technology can take them into account, with the long-term aim of supporting people’s own memory.

Human-centered Machine Perception Design

Perceptive systems only help when they are designed around the people and settings they are meant for. In this line we study how such systems should be designed and evaluated: when people rely on AI support, how technology can support self-reflection on social interactions, and how stakeholders can be involved through co-design. We are also exploring language-model-based microsimulations as a method for evaluating designs across users and contexts.

Common Foundations

All three lines rest on shared datasets, tools, and methodology. This includes multimodal corpora, open-source research infrastructure such as the OpenVIMO platform for large-scale online experiments with videoconferencing, and conceptual work on how to handle ambiguity, subjectivity, and context in data collection and self-report.

Collaborations

We work with colleagues across TU Delft and with external partners in academia and applied research, including the Hybrid Intelligence Centre, VU Amsterdam, Leiden University, and TNO. Where possible we work together with societal stakeholders, so that our research addresses needs in domains such as healthcare and public services.

Open science

We aim to make our tools and data available to the research community. Examples are the OpenVIMO platform and the multimodal datasets described on the publications page.

Working with us

Students at TU Delft. We offer BSc and MSc thesis projects connected to our research lines. If you are interested, send an email with a short description of your background and the topics that appeal to you. See also the teaching page.

Researchers and organisations. We are open to collaborations on context-sensitive affective computing and human-centered AI, including joint projects with partners from healthcare, education, and the public sector. Get in touch by email: b.j.w.dudzik [at] tudelft [dot] nl.
